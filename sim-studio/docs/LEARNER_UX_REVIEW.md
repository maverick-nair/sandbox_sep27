# Learner experience review

A walkthrough of the published learner experience (desktop 1440 px and phone 390 px), from the first screen to the debrief, before this round of changes. Each issue lists what the learner meets, why it matters and what changed.

## Summary

The simulation asked the learner to act before it told them where they were. The first screen was a sign-in form; the organization, the role, the team, the way work flows and the leadership model were never introduced, although the definition already held all of that content (product brief, CEO letter, target message, model briefing, team profiles, stage descriptions, tour steps). The first real screen after sign-in was a modal asking for forty choices at once. The fix is a narrative prologue that sets the scene chapter by chapter, a game-like planning deck, and a guided first look at the workspace, all authored and tailored in GenieKreator.

## Issues and resolutions

| # | Where | Issue | Why it matters | Resolution |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | First screen | A sign-in form (name, nickname, cohort, group) is the first thing the learner sees. | Asks for action before any context; feels like an assessment login. | A cover screen introduces the company and the role and offers one action: begin the briefing. Sign-in moves to the end of the prologue, framed as accepting the role. Language is chosen on the cover so the story is read in the learner's language. |
| 2 | Story | The product brief, the target message and the leadership briefing were never shown. The CEO letter was hidden in a collapsed box. | Learners do not know what the company sells, why this quarter matters or what good leadership means here. | Chapter 1 "Welcome to the company" (brief and fact cards), Chapter 2 "Your mission" (the letter, opened like an envelope, with the target, the weeks and the team as mission cards). |
| 3 | Funnel | Stage names (Sales lead, Qualify, Proposal...) appear everywhere, but the idea that work flows from one stage to the next, and that conversions come out of the last one, is never explained. | The main mechanic (a stuck stage starves the rest) is invisible. | Chapter 3 "How the work flows": the stages as a connected pipeline with work flowing through it; tapping a stage shows what it does and who works there. |
| 4 | Team | Learners meet ten people for the first time inside the planning modal, as one line each. | Reading people is the core skill; there was no moment to do it. | Chapter 4 "Meet your team": face-down cards the learner turns over one by one (background, experience, first impression). At least three must be read to continue (author setting), echoing the legacy rule. |
| 5 | Model | The four styles were explained in one line each, with no link to skill and morale. | Learners guess, then feel judged. | Chapter 5 "How to lead them": the skill and morale grid with a flip card for each style, then an unscored practice round of two quick matches with feedback. |
| 6 | Rules | Weekly rhythm, cost of actions in days, consequences, rethinks and how the final score is made were only in a "How it works" panel found later. | Unclear rules read as unfair outcomes. | Chapter 6 "How the simulation works": the week as five step cards, the score breakdown with the author's weights, and three tips. Still available later from "How it works". |
| 7 | Week 1 plan | Ten people times four styles: forty buttons in one modal, before the workspace is visible. | Overwhelming; encourages clicking through. | A planning deck: one person at a time, a hand of four style cards to play, an avatar strip to jump between people, and a "see everyone" grid for later weeks. Week 1 asks "How will you read each person this week?" |
| 8 | Workspace | No orientation to the inbox, the team, actions costing days or ending the day. | Learners do not know where to look or how time moves. | A five-step guided look at the workspace after the first plan (skippable, author can switch off), highlighting each area in turn. |
| 9 | Inbox | Two near-identical welcome items from the CEO (the letter and the first decision). | Reads as a duplicate. | The letter already read in the prologue is marked as read and titled "Welcome letter"; the first decision stays as the only thing needing a reply. |
| 10 | Friday wrap-up | The header behind the wrap-up already shows next week's Monday. | Confusing sense of time. | The header shows "Week N · Friday review" while the wrap-up is open. |
| 11 | Toasts | Achievement and consequence toasts sit bottom right and on phones cover the Rethink button. | Blocks a key action. | Toasts move to the top, below the header, stay shorter and can be dismissed. |
| 12 | Motion | Screens change without transitions; nothing feels like a game. | Low engagement. | Chapter transitions, card deals and flips, the envelope, pipeline flow, the style card "played" onto a person, and count-ups in the wrap-up. All motion stops for learners who ask for reduced motion. |

## What GenieKreator authors control

- A new Studio section, **Learner onboarding** (Build), lists the chapters in order. Each can be switched off, reordered and retitled, with its lead line and (for the company chapter) its fact cards; authors can add their own chapters, for example the client's values or a word from their sponsor.
- Settings: how many team cards learners must turn over, the practice round, and the workspace tour.
- Chapter text uses the same context fields as the rest of the simulation, so it follows every tailoring, and it is translatable with the other texts.
- The creation flow's review step shows what learners see first.
- Health checks: onboarding switched off entirely, a custom chapter without text, empty fact cards, and more cards to turn over than people on the team, each with a suggested fix.
- "Preview onboarding" opens the learner experience at the cover.
